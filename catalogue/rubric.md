# Grading

The project carries the whole grade of the Maude block, which is 2.5 of the
10 points of the course.

## Weights

| Component | Weight |
|---|---|
| Maude specification (structure, equations vs. rules, comments, example term) | 25% |
| Properties stated (part 3 of the statement) | 10% |
| Verification: `search` and the LTL model checker (part 4) | 25% |
| Mutations (part 5) | 20% |
| Rules-to-equations optimization (part 6) | 5% |
| Report (coherence, justification of simplifications, depth of the development) | 15% |

This is a team grade, weighted by the task split the team declares in its
report: for each of parts 1 to 6, who led it, and an approximate contribution
percentage per member, adding up to 100, agreed and signed by the whole team.
An uneven split declared honestly is not penalized. The instructor may adjust
the split if the defense does not support it.

## The defense is a gate, not a component

The oral defense has no row in the table. It carries no marks of its own, but
passing it is a necessary condition for passing the project: a student who
does not pass it does not pass the project, however good the submitted work,
and loses the 2.5 points of the block. It does not fail the course.

The defense is assessed per student, so a defense showing that a member does
not understand what they submitted can place that student below the rest of
the team, or fail them outright, independently of the breakdown above.

The reason it carries no weight: a weighted defense is a quantity a student
can trade against the deliverables. A member who contributed little can
accept a weak defense if the team's specification is strong enough to absorb
it. Removing the weight removes the trade.

## Grading environment

Projects are graded with Maude 3.5. Students may work with any version, but
are told to check before submitting that their specification loads without
warnings and gives the same results in 3.5.
